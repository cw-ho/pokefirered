# HUSH — Story Bible (working draft)

> A crime-noir Pokémon story built on the FireRed engine.
> Two moles on opposite sides of the law, one sedative flooding the streets,
> and a causeway nobody crosses clean.

Status: **draft v0.1**. Everything here is up for debate. Names marked *(tbc)* are placeholders.

---

## 1. Tone

- **Noir, not grimdark.** Quiet, tired, rain on concrete. Violence happens mostly off-screen; the weight comes from consequences.
- **Local, not cliché.** It should feel like home to anyone from the region through texture — the 5am checkpoint queue, a kopitiam-lit corridor at night, the hum of an aircon compressor — never through jokes, landmarks or Singlish punchlines.
- **Nobody is fully clean.** Every major character has a lie they're protecting.
- **The drug is never glamorised.** We show the people around it.

---

## 2. The World

### The Republic of Halcyon (the city-state)
Small, dense, orderly. Everything works, everyone is watched. Drug laws are brutal: carry over the threshold and your life is effectively over. People call it **"the Island"**.

### Across (the mainland state)
Officially *Seberang Province* — but everyone just calls it **Across**. Bigger, looser, cheaper. Where the Island's money goes to hide, and where the labs and stash houses are.

### The Link
The causeway between them. A single road, a single rail line, and the **Link Checkpoint** at each end. Hundreds of thousands cross daily. The whole game orbits this one stretch of road.

### The Strait
The water around the Link. Fishing boats, patrol boats, and things moving at night that shouldn't be.

## 4. The Drug: Hush

- **What it is:** etomidate — in-world, a fast-acting sedative used at Pokémon Centers and vet clinics to put injured Pokémon under for treatment.
- **Where it comes from:** stock diverted from **Silvane Pharmaceuticals**, plus cruder batches cooked in labs Across.
- **What's sold:** vape pods laced with it. Street name **Hush**. Sold as "just chill pods" through group chats, to teenagers mostly.
- **What it does to people (shown, not lectured):** heavy limbs, blank stares, falling over at bus stops. Kids who stop showing up. A Pokémon dragging its unconscious trainer home.
- **What it does to Pokémon:** in strong doses it's used to *break* wild Pokémon — sedate them so poachers can catch and sell them. A second business line for the syndicate.

### Supply chain (= the mission structure)
1. **Source** — Silvane warehouse leaks (Meridian Central) / labs Across
2. **Crossing** — hidden in lorries over the Link, or by boat over the Strait
3. **Storage** — Calder Tunnels, Pasir Quay containers
4. **Distribution** — Lantern Row fronts → runners → group chats
5. **Money** — laundered through the *Meridian Star* and shell companies in Meridian Central

## 5. Cast

### The Player — *"the Ghost"*
Top cadet at Fort Calder Academy. Pulled aside by Inspector Pereira and publicly **expelled for assault** — a staged disgrace. Sent into the syndicate as a low-level runner. Only Pereira knows the truth. Syndicate codename: **Ghost**.

### The Rival — Kai Lim *(default name, renameable)*
Grew up next door in Ashgrove. Always one step ahead of you. Secretly **raised by the syndicate** and planted in the Academy as a teenager. After you're "expelled", Kai graduates top of the class and joins **Narcotics**. Kai is the syndicate's mole in the police.
- *Arc:* Kai starts loyal to the syndicate, starts wanting out, and ends up trying to erase their own past — by any means.

### Inspector Leonard Pereira — the Handler
Late 50s, tired, decent. Your only lifeline and the only proof you're a cop. Calls you on a burner phone. **Killed at the end of Act 2.** After that, nobody can prove who you are.

### Vincent Yeo — the Boss
Owner of *Yeo Brothers Logistics*. Soft-spoken, generous to charities, always remembers your mum's birthday. Runs the syndicate (**the Harbour Line**) like a family business. Never raises his voice.

### Samsul "Sam" Rahman — the Lieutenant
Runs the street side. Takes you under his wing like a little brother. Genuinely likes you. **Also** planning to take Yeo down — and wants to use you to do it.

### Deputy Commissioner Adrian Teo — the Chief
Public face of the anti-drug campaign. On TV every week. Kai's mentor. **Secretly Yeo's most valuable asset** — the person above Kai in the chain.

### Priya Nair / "Pixel" — the Informant
Runs a phone-repair shop in Lantern Row. Sells information to the police, to the syndicate — and to a **third party** nobody knows about yet.

### The Kingfisher Crew — the Third Party
Young, fast, from Across. Sell Hush entirely through apps, no fronts, no loyalty. They want the Harbour Line's routes. Arrive in Act 2.

### Nadia Hassan — the Gym Leader of Mangrove Bay
Looks bent — takes syndicate money, waves boats through. Actually **another undercover officer**, run by a different handler. Neither of you knows about the other. You can blow each other's cover by accident.

### Professor Raymond Seah — the Oak role
Respected vet anaesthesiologist, gives you your first Pokémon. Twenty years ago, at Sera Research Station, **he developed the original Hush formula** for safe Pokémon sedation. He's never told anyone it got out.

### Your Mum
Lives in Ashgrove. Thinks you were really expelled. Every visit home costs you something.

## 7. Plot

### Act 1 — Down (Badges 1–3)
1. **Ashgrove.** Prof. Seah gives you your first Pokémon. Kai picks the one strong against yours.
2. **Fort Calder Academy.** You and Kai top the class. Pereira takes you aside.
3. **The expulsion.** A staged fight. You're thrown out in front of everyone. Kai watches.
4. **Greywater.** You start running small packages for Sam's crew. First crossing over the Link — you don't know what's in the bag. (You do.)
5. **Lumen Vista.** Your first Hush deal, to kids not much younger than you. You see what it does.
6. **Pasir Quay / *Meridian Star*.** Sam vouches for you. You meet Yeo at a party on the cruise ship.
7. **Meanwhile:** news reports show Kai graduating with honours, joining Narcotics under DC Teo.

**End of Act 1:** Yeo tells his inner circle: *"There's a police rat in this family."* At the same time, Teo tells Narcotics: *"Yeo has someone inside our unit."* Both moles are now being hunted — by each other.

### Act 2 — Deeper (Badges 4–6)
1. **Lantern Row.** You're promoted. The Golden Lantern basement — the syndicate's real HQ.
2. **The Kingfisher Crew** start hitting Harbour Line runners. Turf war.
3. **Mercy Hill.** A runner you trained ODs. Funeral at the columbarium. Mum is there — it's a neighbour's kid.
4. **Mangrove Bay.** Night drop at the reserve. Nadia waves the boat through. You think she's dirty. She thinks you're dirty.
5. **Calder Tunnels.** Kai leads a raid you tipped off — but someone tipped the syndicate too. Both moles in the same tunnel, neither knows.
6. **Sam's offer:** help me take Yeo down. Trust me.
7. **The Link.** Pereira sets up a meet Across, where Island police can't follow. He gets there. So does someone else.

**End of Act 2:** **Pereira is killed.** His burner phone is gone. Your file is gone. You are, officially, just a criminal.

### Act 3 — The Rooftop (Badges 7–8 + endgame)
1. **Alone.** Your burner rings. Unknown number. Someone has Pereira's phone.
2. **Sera Island.** You find the origin of Hush — and Prof. Seah's name on it.
3. **Across.** The labs. The Kingfishers. Priya's third buyer revealed.
4. **The truth about Teo.** DC Teo was Yeo's man all along — and Kai's handler.
5. **Kai breaks.** Kai wants out, and starts deleting everyone who knows what they are — including Yeo.
6. **Silvane Pharmaceuticals.** Everyone converges on the tower. **Rooftop face-off with Kai.**

## 8. Endings

Driven by choices and a hidden **Trust/Suspicion** score.

| Ending | How | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Infernal** | Default / most choices compromised | You expose Teo, but Kai walks free in uniform. You get your badge back and nobody's clean. The original film's ending. |
| **Daylight** | Saved Nadia's cover, kept Pereira's evidence, refused Hush | Kai is arrested, Teo goes down, you testify. You go home to Ashgrove. It costs you everything else. |
| **Boss** | Sided with Sam, used Hush, betrayed Yeo | You take over the Harbour Line. Last scene: your phone rings. It's Kai, still a cop, asking for instructions. |

### Rival idea: the credit-taker (new)
- Whatever progress the hero makes busting the druglords **somehow ends up credited to the rival**.
- The hero **works with the rival** and **lets him take all the credit** — the hero can't be seen (cover / record / he's "nobody"), the rival needs the wins.
- The public sees the rival as the hero of the drug war; the real work is done by a washed-up nobody in the shadows.
- Pairs naturally with Hero C: one got the dream, one got the gutter — and the gutter is doing the real work.

Questions to explore:
- [ ] Does the rival know he's taking stolen credit, or is it arranged by someone above (Teo? the League)?
- [ ] Is the rival clean, compromised, or secretly the syndicate's man? (Can combine with Version 1 / 2.)
- [ ] Why does the hero accept it — protection, money, friendship, guilt?
- [ ] When does the hero stop letting him?

### Hero vs. everyone (new)
- Over the course of the story the hero ends up **fighting all three sides**: the **League**, the **syndicate**, and the **police**.
- Nobody is fully on his side — every faction is at some point an enemy.
- Ideas for how each fight could happen:
  - **League** — Gym Leaders on the payroll, the League protecting its golden boy (the rival), the Elite Four.
  - **Syndicate** — his own crew when his cover cracks, the Kingfishers, Yeo's enforcers.
  - **Police** — Narcotics raids, checkpoint officers, Teo's people hunting the informant.
- [ ] Could make a nice structure: each act is dominated by a different side turning on him?

### Across (JB) unlocks after the Elite Four (new)
- The mainland is **locked until the hero beats the Elite Four**.
- So the Elite Four stays — and **Across becomes the post-League part of the game** (like Johto/Kanto in Gold).
- Implications to work out:
  - Before the E4, the Link is a place you can reach but not cross — checkpoint scenes, smuggling handoffs, chases that stop at the border.
  - Scenes in the draft set Across (Pereira's meet, the labs, the Kingfishers' base) either move to the Island side, or move post-E4.
  - [x] Is Across the **true final act** (story continues after E4), or **post-game**? → **True final act** (leaning).
  - [ ] Why does beating the E4 open the border — status, a League pass, the rival's access, someone wants him over there?

### The real man behind the syndicate is Across (new)
- The **true head of the syndicate lives Across**. Whoever runs things on the Island (Yeo in the current draft) is only the local operator.
- Beating the E4 is when the hero **finally has his reason to cross** — he's learned who's really at the top, and the only way to end it is to go over there.
- So the Elite Four becomes the **midpoint / turning point**, not the ending. Across is the final act.
- Ideas:
  - The Island fight (Yeo, Teo, the rival) only cuts off one arm. The source — the labs, the money, the real boss — is Across.
  - The reveal of the real boss could come right after (or during) the E4 / Champion moment — at the height of the rival's fame.
  - Across is where Island police have **no jurisdiction** — the hero goes in with no badge, no backup, nobody on his side.
- [ ] Who is the real boss? (Someone we've already met? Connected to the hero's past, the rival's father, Prof. Seah?) — *KIV*
- [ ] Does anyone follow him over — the rival, Nadia, Sam? — *KIV*

### The hero is originally from Across (new — twist)
- The hero **was born and raised Across**, not on the Island.
- For some reason he ended up on the Island — and has kept it quiet / half-forgotten / buried.
- So when Across finally unlocks after the E4, he's not going somewhere new — **he's going home**.
- He has his own reason to go back, separate from (or tangled up with) the real syndicate boss being there.
- Possible flavours (unfiltered):
  - He fled Across as a kid / teen — something happened there.
  - He was sent over — by family, or by someone who wanted him away.
  - He came over for the League dream (the Island League is the big one) and never went back.
  - His family is still over there — or what's left of it.
  - The real boss knows exactly who he is.
- Conflicts with the "grew up in Ashgrove next to the rival" idea → could become: arrived on the Island young, *then* grew up next to the rival. Keep both open.

### What badges actually mean (new)
Badges should be more than a ticket to the Elite Four. In a crime noir, a badge is **power, access, or a debt**. Unfiltered options — can mix:

- **Badges = legal licences.** On the Island, League badges carry real legal weight. More badges = more rights: carry battle-ready Pokémon in public, enter restricted zones (port, labs, checkpoint lanes), give testimony, act as a licensed bounty trainer. Eight badges + E4 = the **League Pass** — free movement across the Link. *(This is why Across unlocks after the E4.)*
- **Badges = endorsements.** A badge means that Gym Leader **vouches for you**. Clean leaders' badges open honest doors; dirty leaders' badges come with strings — you now owe them. Collecting badges = building a network of favours and debts.
- **Badges = access to secrets.** Every Gym Leader is holding something (records, a ledger, a name). Beating them — or what you do around the fight — is how you get it. Badges double as a trail of evidence.
- **Badges = underworld currency.** In the battle rings and the syndicate, a badge-holder is worth more: paid more, trusted with bigger jobs, harder for police to touch.
- **Badges replace HMs as "access".** FireRed already gates field moves by badges — reskin that: instead of Cut/Surf/Strength, each badge unlocks a **place** (port gates, Silvane loading bay, checkpoint lanes, the Strait at night).
- **The Elite Four = the people who run the Island.** Not just strong trainers — a council of power: e.g. a police commissioner, a pharma CEO, a politician, a media owner. Challenging them = **getting into the room** where decisions are made. Champion = a seat at the table, untouchable. *That's* why the syndicate wants its own man (the rival) as the face of the League.
- **The badge that matters most is the one he already has.** Badge 1 from ten years ago — the last honest thing he ever earned. (Ties to the badge-case riff.)

### Riffs (unfiltered — from brainstorm)
1. **The badge case.** Game opens with the hero finding Badge 1 in a drawer — case with seven empty slots. Each badge refills it. Final shot = the case, full or not, depending on the ending.
2. **The rival's speeches.** Every time the rival takes credit there's a TV press conference describing what *you* did, with small lies. Later, one lie is how you realise something's wrong.
3. **The starter.** Old, slow, can't (or won't) evolve. Finally evolves when the hero stops hiding — or dies. Either way, a gut-punch.
4. **Three records on the trainer card.** Police record / syndicate rank / League standing side by side, changing over the story. Nobody sees all of you.
5. **Why he quit is tied to the rival.** The rival's smooth path was paid for by someone, at the hero's expense. Makes "letting him take the credit" hurt more later.
6. **The Champion moment is stolen.** You beat the E4, but the rival is crowned / the fight is covered up / the record says you were never there. Erased at your highest point — that's what sends you Across.
